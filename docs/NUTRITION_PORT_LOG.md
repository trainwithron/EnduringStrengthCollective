# Port log: Ron's Mix & Macros recipe engine (Nutrition phase 4a)

Source: `docs/old-app/enduring-strength-checkin-engine.html` (Ron's own file, kept outside the repo).
Port: `lib/meal-templates/` (nothing in the running app calls it yet; the builder in phase 4b does).

This log is for Ron to read. It lists **everything that differs from the old app**, **every check that was run**, and **what needs Ron's decision**. Nothing was redesigned.

## 1. What was copied, and how it was proved identical

| Part | Old app lines | Ported to | Edited? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food table (150 foods) and unit weights | 709-715, 726-897 | `food-table.ts` | No |
| Diet table per food, name-to-food table | 915-997, 1003-1033 | `food-names.ts` | No (4 names added, see 2.2) |
| 66 recipes (20 breakfast, 19 lunch, 19 dinner, 8 snack) | 1170-2259 | `recipes.ts` | One recipe (see 2.1) |

The recipe file is **generated by copying the old file's lines**, not retyped. To prove it, every ported formula was run next to the old one at **172,788 combinations of protein, carbs and fat** (protein 0 to 160 g, carbs 0 to 260 g, fat 0 to 80 g). The outputs (food, amount, unit, line text) were identical for 65 of the 66 recipes. The 66th is the string cheese snack, fixed on purpose (2.1). The food table, the name table and the diet table were also compared and are identical.

## 2. Every change

### 2.1 The one recipe formula that was fixed

**`s_string_cheese_jerky_apple` (String Cheese, Beef Jerky & Apple).** The recipe counts string cheese in **sticks**, but the food table holds string cheese **per gram**, and the formula multiplied the per-gram numbers by the number of sticks, so each stick counted as 1 g. A real stick is 24 g, so the old formula undercounted the cheese's protein and fat by 24 times and the snack came out more than double its protein target (42 g of protein for a 20 g target). Fixed by multiplying by the stick weight (`UNIT_WEIGHT_G.pieces`, 24 g) in the four places the recipe uses it. After the fix the snack lands inside the tolerance at every size on every plain-diet target.

**Also, cosmetic only:** six `let` declarations in the copied text (`slices` five times, `dryOatsG` once) became `const`, because the project's build treats a `let` that is never reassigned as an error. No behaviour changes: the 172,788-build comparison above was run again after this change and still differs only for the string cheese recipe.

### 2.2 Additions to the tables

Four ingredient names that the recipes print were never in the old name table, so they could not be looked up for macros: **Skim Milk, Liquid Egg Whites, 0% Greek Yogurt, String Cheese**. They are mapped to their foods in `EXTRA_NAME_TO_KEY`. The old table is untouched.

### 2.3 How a recipe is aimed at a meal (the one new piece of engine)

The old formulas aim at their **main** foods only. A fixed side (fruit, vegetables, tempeh's own carbs, the carbs in a milk) adds macros the formula never subtracts, so many recipes came out well over target: run as written, only **39 of 66** recipes landed inside the tolerance on at least half of the target sizes tested. Rather than rewrite 60 formulas, the engine (`scale.ts`) calls each formula **unchanged**, measures what the meal really contains (every line, from the food table), and re-aims the formula by the amount it missed by, up to eight passes. With that, **65 of 66** land on at least half of the sizes tested in their best target shape (the 66th is switched off, 2.7).

A recipe that cannot land for a particular target is **skipped for that target and never shown**.

### 2.4 Lines too small to show

A recipe prints nothing for a food whose amount is tiny (for example butter under 2 g). The old app still did the arithmetic with it. The port treats a line with **no text** as **not part of the meal**: it is not shown and not counted in the macros. A formula that asks for a negative amount at an extreme target (the oats recipe at zero carbs) is read as zero.

### 2.5 The tolerance

The design's numbers are used as written: calories and protein within 10 percent, protein never below 90 percent of the target, carbs and fat within 15 percent. **One addition:** carbs and fat also pass when they are within **5 g**, because 15 percent of a 6 g target is under a gram, finer than a food table can promise (keto and carnivore slots have a handful of carb grams).

### 2.6 Diets

All seven diets the old app declared are kept, including pescatarian (8 recipes), which was already in the old recipes. Pescatarian is also in the client's Preferences and the allergen and diet checks (phase 3). The old app's "no recipe for this diet and slot: show another slot's recipe" fallback is **not** carried over: it showed a lunch under "breakfast". Instead the gap is reported (section 4) and the builder fills it from the coach's own recipes or the AI top-up.

### 2.7 One recipe is switched off

`s_jerky_eggs_carnivore` (Beef Jerky & Hard-Boiled Eggs): the formula caps the eggs at three and jerky has almost no fat, so the snack holds about 16 g of fat at most, while a keto or carnivore snack aims for 18 to 50 g. It misses at every size. It stays in the file (so it is easy to bring back) and is on the disabled list with this reason (`disabled.ts`).

### 2.8 Things the recipes mention but do not count

Some recipes name small seasonings in the preparation text only: sugar-free teriyaki sauce, red onion, lime juice, cinnamon, turmeric, nutritional yeast, sea salt. They have no line, no amount and no macros in the old app and none here. They are negligible, but a coach should know the allergen tags read the recipe's **name and its ingredient lines**, not the preparation text: the Pineapple Chicken Teriyaki dinner is tagged soy (from "Teriyaki" in its name) and sesame (from its sesame oil line), while a spice named only in the preparation text is not seen.

## 3. Checks that now run on every change (`lib/meal-templates/diagnostics.test.ts`)

- 66 recipes, 20 / 19 / 19 / 8 by slot, unique ids, every recipe has a real diet, a name, keywords.
- The food table: 150 foods, no food weighs more than itself per gram, every key in the diet and name tables is a real food.
- Every recipe built at the old app's five extreme targets **and** across the whole spread: nothing throws, no NaN, every printed ingredient is a known food.
- A scaled meal has no negative or empty line, its macros are the sum of **all** shown lines, and it is inside the tolerance.
- Every enabled recipe lands on **at least 3 of the 6 sizes** in its best target shape; a disabled one lands on none and has a written reason.
- The coverage gaps in section 4 are an exact list: a recipe that closes a gap, or a change that opens one, fails the test until the list is updated on purpose.
- Allergen and diet tags are **worked out from the ingredients** (never typed): every recipe fits every diet it declares (vegan has no animal food, vegetarian no meat or fish, pescatarian no meat, and the old food-by-diet table agrees).
- The renderer keeps each recipe's own line text and a metric client sees no ounce hints.

**Target shapes tested.** Sizes from a small snack (15 g protein) to a big dinner (100 g). Each diet is tested on its own carb and fat shape: standard (balanced split), low carb, high carb, keto (a few grams of carbs, fat above protein), carnivore (no carbs, fat about equal to protein).

## 4. What the starter library can and cannot fill

32 of the diet / slot / target combinations have **no** recipe that lands (the exact list is in the test). In plain words:

- **Pescatarian breakfast**: nothing on a standard or high-carb plan (both pescatarian breakfasts are fatty fish, and their fat is too high for those targets); one on a low-carb plan.
- **Pescatarian snack** on a low-carb plan, and most sizes on a high-carb plan.
- **Carnivore snack**: none (the one recipe is switched off, 2.7).
- **Keto**: large breakfasts (1.6 times the base and up) and snacks above a small size.

Also thin (fewer than three meals): most vegan and vegetarian lunches, most keto slots, pescatarian dinners. This is what the Generate button (AI top-up, phase 5) and the coach's own saved meals are for; the library alone gives a three-option menu for omnivore and vegetarian breakfast, and for omnivore lunch, dinner and snack.

Counts of enabled recipes per diet and slot: omnivore 17 / 15 / 15 / 6, vegetarian 13 / 3 / 3 / 4, vegan 2 / 3 / 3 / 1, keto 2 / 3 / 5 / 2, paleo 4 / 7 / 8 / 3, pescatarian 2 / 3 / 2 / 1, carnivore 1 / 1 / 1 / 0 (breakfast / lunch / dinner / snack).

## 5. The food table against real food data (USDA, 150 foods)

Each food was compared with the closest USDA record (per 100 g; foods counted in whole units were converted at the weights the old app uses: egg 50 g, sourdough slice 40 g, whole-wheat slice 28 g, white slice 25 g, bagel 100 g, wrap 50 g, rice cake 9 g). A number is listed when it is **more than 8 percent and more than 1.5 g per 100 g** away from USDA. **No value in the table was changed**: these are for you to accept or correct.

**No USDA record found in the stored data (cannot be checked here):** chicken breast, chicken tenderloin (no "meat only, raw" record), whey isolate, casein, pea protein, TVP, brown-rice pasta (supplements and some branded foods are not in the stored set). Chicken thigh and drumstick exist but are leaner in USDA (protein 19.7 and 19.4, fat 4.1 and 3.7) than the table (20 / 8 and 19 / 9): the table's chicken is fattier.
